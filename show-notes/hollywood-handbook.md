# Hollywood Handbook: notes for the Spotter

- **Sean and Hayes's own host reads: the owner keeps the riffing, not the read.** Mark
  each read whole, from first ad word to last, as `sponsor`. Then decide by whether it
  has a bit in it:
  - **A read with a bit stays in: `medium`.** A bit is a joke, an absurd or deadpan
    aside, or riffing that goes somewhere the ad copy never would (e.g. explaining in
    painful detail how you eat the meat, then "I'm a pescatarian"). It has to be a
    clear step outside the pitch, at least a sentence or two. A slightly odd or playful
    phrase inside a sentence of ad copy is still ad copy.
  - **A straight read is cut: `high`.** Nothing but ad copy from start to finish:
    product claims, prices, offers, the URL or code, fine print. Scripted openers that
    set up the pitch ("Remember when...") and one-line sign-offs ("Trust me, it makes
    life easier") are ad copy, not a bit.
  - **Unclear whether there's a bit: `medium`.**
- **How to recognize their reads:** a promo code or link with "handbook" in it, or a
  first-person read ("I", "we've been using..."). Either way, the bit-or-no-bit test
  above decides the confidence.
- **Still cut at `high`:** reads where someone else introduces themselves ("Hey, it's
  Kelly Rowland", "This is Gemma Spegg from..."), stitched-in ads with no first person
  at all, trailers for other shows, and "This is an iHeart Podcast. Guaranteed human."
- **Breaks are stitched in mid-conversation,** often with no lead-in or return line. The
  in point is the first word of the first ad and the out point is the last word of the
  last ad.
